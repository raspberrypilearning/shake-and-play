## Play on your micro:bit

Download your code to the micro:bit and play it away from the computer.


> [!TASK]
>
> Plug the small end of the USB cable into the micro:bit. Connect the other end to your computer.
>
> ![A USB cable plugged into the micro:bit.](images/connect-usb.jpg){:width="450px"}

> [!TASK]
>
> Click **Download** at the bottom of the MakeCode editor.
>
> ![The Download button in the MakeCode editor.](images/download-button.png){:width="300px"}

The first time you download, MakeCode asks you to pair your micro:bit with the computer.

> [!TASK]
>
> Click **Next**.
>
> ![The MakeCode window that says Connect your micro:bit to your computer, with a Next button.](images/connect1.png){:width="450px"}

> [!TASK]
>
> Click **Pair**.
>
> ![The MakeCode window that says Pair your micro:bit to your browser, with a Pair button.](images/connect2.png){:width="450px"}

> [!TASK]
>
> Select your micro:bit, then click **Connect**.
>
> ![The browser window asking to connect, with the BBC micro:bit selected and a Connect button.](images/connect3.png){:width="450px"}

> [!TASK]
>
> Click **Download**.
>
> ![The MakeCode window that says Connected to micro:bit, with a Download button.](images/connect4.png){:width="450px"}

> [!TASK]
>
> Plug the battery pack into the micro:bit. Keep it plugged in, even when the USB cable is in.

> [!TASK]
>
> Hold the USB plug, not the cable, and gently pull it out of the micro:bit.
>
> ![A hand holding the USB plug and pulling it out of the micro:bit.](images/unplug.png){:width="450px"}

> [!TIP]
>
> Use the USB cable for downloading code, then take it out to test. Testing with the cable plugged in can break the USB port.

**Test:** Shake the micro:bit to play your sound.

> [!TASK]
>
> Experiment with other movements and download to the board.
>
> ![The drop-down menu in the on shake block, showing movements like logo up, screen down and tilt left.](images/more-than-shake.png){:width="450px"}
